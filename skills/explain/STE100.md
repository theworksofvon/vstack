# ASD-STE100 digest

ASD-STE100, Simplified Technical English, is a controlled language: a short
dictionary where each word has one meaning, plus writing rules that cap
sentence length and verb forms. The full specification is about 400 pages
and free from asd-ste100.org; this page is the working subset.

## The dial

Callers pass a percentage. It selects which rule groups apply:

| dial | groups | reads like |
|---|---|---|
| 100% (default) | A + B + C | a maintenance manual |
| 80% | A + B | plain technical writing with STE grammar |
| 50% | A | clear prose for a general reader |

## Group A: structure

- One topic per paragraph, topic sentence first, six sentences at most.
- Twenty words per sentence at most in instructions, twenty-five in
  description. Count them.
- One instruction per sentence. Two only when they happen at the same time.
- Active voice. The actor comes first: "the scheduler moves the task", never
  "the task is moved".
- Vertical lists for sequences of more than two steps.
- Numerals for numbers.
- A warning or caution comes before the step it protects, as a command.
- Define a term the reader may not know in one short sentence the first
  time it appears, then use that exact term every time. One name per thing.
- Literal language. An idiom, metaphor, or figure of speech gets replaced by
  the fact it stood for.

## Group B: grammar

Verb forms allowed: infinitive, imperative, simple present, simple past,
future with "will", and past participle as an adjective ("the closed
valve"). That excludes:

- The -ing form as a verb or noun. "Starting the service" becomes "When you
  start the service". "The running process" becomes "the process that runs".
- Present perfect and progressive. "Has been sent" becomes "was sent".
- Passive in instructions.
- Contractions.

Other rules:

- Keep articles and demonstratives: "the", "a", "this". Telegraphic style
  drops them; STE keeps them.
- Noun clusters: three nouns at most in a row. "Consumer group rebalance
  protocol" becomes "the protocol that rebalances a consumer group".
- Keep "that" in relative clauses: "the file that the job writes".
- "Can" means possible, "must" means required, "will" is the future only.
  "May" and "might" are out.
- Negation is "do not", "does not", "is not", with no contraction.

## Group C: dictionary

Only approved words, each in its one approved meaning and part of speech,
plus technical names and technical verbs from the domain. A domain noun
("partition", "offset", "pointer") counts as a technical name and is
allowed, so long as it is used as a noun and means one thing throughout.

Common substitutions. The left word is out; use the right one.

| out | in |
|---|---|
| ensure, make certain | make sure |
| perform, carry out, execute | do |
| utilize, employ | use |
| commence, initiate | start |
| terminate, cease | stop |
| indicate, display (verb) | show |
| sufficient, adequate | enough |
| require | need, must |
| prior to, following | before, after |
| in order to | to |
| via, by means of | with, through |
| obtain, acquire | get |
| approximately | about |
| additional | more |
| modify, alter | change |
| verify, confirm | make sure, check |
| attempt | try |
| assist | help |
| occur, take place | happen |
| regarding, concerning | about |
| however | but |
| therefore, thus, hence | so |
| whilst, during the time that | while |

One meaning per word, held throughout the document. "Follow" means come
after, never obey. "Close" is a verb, never "close to". "Test" is a noun;
the verb is "do a test". "Fall" means drop, never "fall back". "Check"
means examine, never a tick mark.

## Self-check before delivery

Read the draft once for each line:

1. A sentence over the word cap.
2. A word that ends in -ing used as a verb or a noun (Group B).
3. A passive construction.
4. A word from the "out" column (Group C).
5. A term used two ways, or two terms for one thing.

Fix every hit. A clean pass is the completion criterion.
